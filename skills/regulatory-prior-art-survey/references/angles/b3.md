# Angle b3 — platform and intermediary-obligation retrieval

- **Conditional:** `business.platform.type != "none"`. **Anchor:** `business.platform.type`.
- **Searches these axes:** `instrument`, `platform-role`.
- **Sources:** `eu-cellar`, `ec-digital-strategy`. **Fallback:** `ec-digital-strategy`.
- **Cap:** 15 — **ordering:** gatekeeper designation, then obligation breadth.

## Not tautological

`regulatory.applies` says nothing about platform role. A regulated product that intermediates
nothing falsifies this predicate.

## Mechanism

Retrieve the instruments that bind a party **because it intermediates between other parties**.

Map `platform.type` onto the instruments' OWN defined roles — intermediary service, hosting
service, online platform, gatekeeper, online intermediation service, data holder — then retrieve
only the obligations attaching to the role the product occupies.

**`claim` carries the instrument's OWN definition of the role, with the text it rests on in
`evidence_quote`; which role this product occupies goes in the candidate's `notes`.** `claim` is
what the document says and never what the system must do. The tiered obligations differ enormously
by role, and a product placed one tier up acquires duties it does not have.

## A caution about ordinary language

A scope can intermediate in plain English — a telehealth service sits between patients and
clinicians — without occupying any role these instruments define. The declared field is what the
predicate reads, and the role determination is what the extraction rests on. Neither is the
everyday sense of the word.

## Why this cap

The intermediary-obligation instruments are a named, closed set. Sized against that set.

## What only this angle covers

Notice-and-action, trader traceability, ranking transparency, terms-change notice periods, and
data-access and interoperability duties. None is reachable from any privacy, security or
accessibility instrument.
