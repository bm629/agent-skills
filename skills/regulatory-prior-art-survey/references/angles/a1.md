# Angle a1 — primary-law register retrieval

- **Conditional:** none — always-on angle.
- **Searches these axes:** `instrument`, `sector`, `jurisdiction`.
- **Sources:** `eu-cellar`, `ecfr-api`, `federal-register-api`, `ico`, `iapp`. **Fallback:** `ecfr-api`.
- **Cap:** 25 — **ordering:** issuing-body authority, then instrument recency.

## Mechanism

Resolve each shortlisted instrument at its issuing body BY ITS OWN IDENTIFIER, and traverse that
instrument's structure — chapter → article → paragraph, or title → part → section. Do not
keyword-search a legal corpus.

**A note on "consolidated", because this instruction used to say it and could not be followed.**
An EU consolidated text has its own identifier — sector 0, with a date suffix — and the `CELEX-`
grammar this survey ships accepts only the as-adopted form. So an angle told to fetch the
consolidated text had no legal id to record it under. Wave 1 therefore carries the act AS ADOPTED
at its own CELEX, and records any consolidation date the served document states in `as_of`, which
stays null when it states none. **Say "as adopted" in the count frame** — calling a sector-3
document consolidated describes a different document from the one fetched. If a later wave needs
consolidated texts, the id grammar widens first.

**Enter by IDENTIFIER, never by site search.** Derive the shortlist from the map's `sector_scoping`
receipt, resolve each instrument to its canonical identifier, fetch at that identifier, walk the
table of contents, and shortlist the divisions that impose a technical or data-handling obligation.

**Record what you identified and deliberately did NOT fetch, in the top-level `notes`.** The
un-fetched remainder is what separates a narrow corpus from a truncated one, and a reader cannot
tell those apart from the fetched half alone. `notes` is the field for a cross-group lead that is
not itself a candidate row, which is exactly what an identified-and-unfetched instrument is.

## The EU channel moved, and the header pair is part of the query

`eur-lex.europa.eu` stopped answering non-JS clients between 2026-08-21 and 2026-09-02 — HTTP 202 with an AWS WAF
JavaScript challenge, to every user agent, on both the `/legal-content/` and `/eli/` forms. Use
`eu-cellar`:

    GET http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/<CELEX>
    Accept: application/xhtml+xml
    Accept-Language: eng

**Both headers are load-bearing.** `text/html` and `application/xml` each return 404 on the same
URI. A query recorded without them cannot be re-run, so record them.

Case law resolves the same way — `62018CJ0311` is a CJEU judgment and answers 200 through this
channel. That is why this survey does not use ECLI: one scheme, one channel, both verified.

## Two required sub-topics

1. **A verdict per sector family**, consuming the map's receipt. Per family, `a1` records either the
   instruments retrieved or a zero-hit cell. A family silently absent from this angle's output is a
   validator failure, not a judgment call.
2. **Product cyber-resilience.** It ships here rather than as its own angle because no anchor
   discriminates it: the instruments bind "products with digital elements", which every archetype
   satisfies, and a conditional angle whose predicate is always true is an always-on angle with
   extra machinery.

## Why this cap

**Sized on the CORPUS, not on a politeness budget.** Its previous justification was EUR-Lex's
`Crawl-delay: 10` — a dead host's manners holding up a live cap — and a delay was never a corpus
ceiling in any case. No product's applicable-instrument set has been observed above ~15; 25 leaves
headroom without inviting padding.

`op.europa.eu/robots.txt` declares `Allow: /` with **no** `Crawl-delay` and nothing disallowing
`/resource/`. Apply a **self-imposed** delay rather than reading silence as permission.

## What only this angle covers

The ONLY angle producing a verbatim, citable, in-force obligation with an article or section
number. Every other angle produces commentary on, or implementation of, what this one retrieves.

## The UK is a recorded gap

`legislation.gov.uk` answers 202 with a zero-byte body to every user agent tried. The UK leg ships
at authority tier 2 through `ico`, and that is a genuine loss of primary-law coverage for one
jurisdiction — recorded on the row, never papered over.
