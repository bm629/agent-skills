# Angle b1 — AI-governance instrument retrieval

- **Conditional:** `data_ml.ml_involvement != "none"`. **Anchor:** `data_ml.ml_involvement`.
- **Searches these axes:** `instrument`, `obligation-dimension`, `model-term`.
- **Sources:** `eu-cellar`, `nist-csrc`, `ec-digital-strategy`. **Fallback:** `nist-csrc`.
- **Cap:** 15 — **ordering:** instrument bindingness (in-force > adopted > proposed), then recency.

## Not tautological

The type trigger is `regulatory.applies`. A scope with `applies: true` and `ml_involvement: none`
falsifies this predicate, so the trigger does not entail it and this angle genuinely gates.

## Why the anchor is `ml_involvement` and not the AI-Act block

`data_ml.eu_ai_act` declares `{applies, risk_level}`, but the block is OPTIONAL and neither
property is required within it. A predicate resting on `risk_level` would fail **closed** for
exactly the products that need this angle — silently, and for every map that omits the block. The
AI-Act fields WIDEN; they never anchor.

## Mechanism

Retrieve the AI-specific instruments and the risk-management frameworks regulators point at, then
**locate the product on the instrument's OWN risk taxonomy** — which is the thing that decides
which obligations attach.

Resolve the AI Act by CELEX through `eu-cellar`, walk its annexes to place the product, then
retrieve only the obligations that classification triggers.

**Do NOT extract high-risk obligations for a product the annexes do not place there.** Record the
classification in the candidate's `claim` and the annex text it rests on in `evidence_quote`. Placing a product one tier too high manufactures
obligations it does not have, and that is as wrong as missing the ones it does.

## Why this cap

The AI-specific instrument set is small and enumerable — the AI Act plus the frameworks regulators
name. Sized against that set, not against the model corpus, which is a different survey's unit.

## What only this angle covers

Obligations that exist only because a component is a model: a risk-management system, data
governance, logging, transparency to the user, human oversight, post-market monitoring. No general
privacy or security instrument imposes these.
