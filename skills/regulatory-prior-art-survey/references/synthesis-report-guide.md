# Writing the synthesis report

`regulatory-register.yaml` is the machine half — **the half that actually travels**, because it
survives into a consumer that never renders the markdown. `report.md` is what a human reads.

## The seven sections are FIXED, in this order

1. **Coverage receipt and status** — written FIRST. A reader must know the shape of the search
   before trusting anything inside it: which angles ran, which did not and why, which registers were
   reached, which jurisdictions were not searched, and which instruments could not be retrieved.
   Carry the standing notice that this survey is not legal advice and that legal review is required.
2. **Applicable instruments** — lens 1, each with the scope condition that made it apply.
3. **Architecture mandates** — lens 2, each with its verification hint and every obligation it
   merged.
4. **Conflicts and open legal questions** — lens 3, plus every requirement read as ambiguous.
   Escalated, never resolved.
5. **Timing obligations** — lens 4, each with the trigger that starts its clock.
6. **Audit evidence and records** — lens 5, grouped by the surface that must produce it.
7. **Enforcement exposure** — statutory maxima cited to their article; published decisions cited to
   the decision.

## What §7 must never contain

**No round number.** "Up to tens of millions" is not a citation.

**No estimate of likelihood.** How probable enforcement is against this product is not something a
document survey can know, and stating it converts a legal maximum into a risk assessment nobody
performed.

**Nothing sourced only from a third-party compilation.** A compilation is a fine way to FIND a
decision and never the thing you cite.

## What the report never does anywhere

**It does not give legal advice, and it does not resolve an ambiguity.** Where the reading was
ambiguous the report says so and routes it to counsel. Where two obligations conflict it presents
both.

**It does not turn a null standard into a standard.** An instrument that says "appropriate" said
"appropriate". Choosing an algorithm on its behalf is the architecture document's decision to make,
with counsel, and it is not this survey's.

**It does not claim the absence of law.** "No applicable instrument found across the angles that ran
and the registers searched" is a search result. "This product is unregulated" is a legal conclusion.

## Grounding

Every obligation carries its instrument, its article and the date the text was true. Every merged
mandate carries every obligation it merged. Every figure in §7 carries its citation.
