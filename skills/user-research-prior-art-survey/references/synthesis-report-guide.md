# Synthesis report — structure guide

Load when running Procedure 4. The report (`report.md`) is the project-level human half;
`evidence-register.yaml` is its machine half. The survey ships both.

## The seven sections are FIXED

1. **Survey scope.** What was searched and how: the angles that ran, the corpora reached, the
   date, and the access barriers hit.
2. **Coverage and absence** *(second on purpose)*. Per angle: ran, vacated or not run, each
   non-`ran` outcome with its cause. Say plainly which questions the survey does not answer.
3. **What the evidence supports.** The convergence and certainty results (lenses 1 and 3),
   grouped by claim, each carrying the certainty of the evidence behind it and the finding ids.
4. **Where studies disagree.** The contradiction result (lens 2), with what differs between them
   — population, platform, or date — rather than a verdict on which is right.
5. **What carries here, and what does not.** The transferability result (lens 4). A
   high-certainty finding measured on a different population and platform belongs in this section
   with its limits stated, not in section 3 unqualified.
6. **Currency.** The recency result (lens 5): which claims rest on an older platform generation,
   and what that means for relying on them now.
7. **Amendments changelog** *(delta runs)*. One dated entry per delta run.

## What does not go in the report

No design recommendation for this product. The survey reports what has been measured and how far
it carries; choosing what to build from that is the downstream document's job, working from the
register. A report that specifies the interface has replaced the decision it was meant to inform.

No pooled statistics. Effect sizes travel verbatim; combining them is meta-analysis and this
survey does not run its methods.

## Grounding

Every claim carries its finding id, its certainty and its transferability. An unqualified claim in
an evidence report is the failure the whole certainty vocabulary exists to prevent.
