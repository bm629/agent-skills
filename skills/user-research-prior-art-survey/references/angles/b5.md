# b5 — developer-experience research retrieval

**Trigger:** conditional — `domain.audience = "developer"` OR `archetype.primary in {cli-tool,
library-sdk, api-service}`. **Anchors:** `domain.audience`, `archetype.primary` — **both
required**, so this trigger has no optional leg at all. **Cap:** 8.
**Scoping note.** The developer-audience disjunct cannot fire alongside this type's trigger
leg 1, which requires a consumer audience (`b2c`/`b2b2c`). That is intended: it serves projects
arriving through leg 2 (`ui.complexity`), while leg-1 developer-tool products are reached by the
archetype disjunct. Recorded as `leg_scope` in the registry so the integrity check reads it as
acknowledged rather than as a dead leg.
**Source:** `stackoverflow-survey`. **Fallback:** `crossref`.

## Mechanism

Retrieve the large-sample developer surveys and their published result sections, plus the
downloadable response dataset where the survey publishes one.

**This angle walks a document, not an index**, and that is what makes it different from every
other angle here. There is one annual survey with a small number of result sections, so the
retrieval is bounded before it starts: enumerate the sections, map each onto the scope's `task`
and `user-population` terms, and record the ones that do not map as searched zeros rather than
omitting them.

## Query strategy

The "query" for a document walk is the traversal: which wave, which result sections, and the
criterion that selected them. Record it that way, verbatim. A section list read and rejected is
a coverage record; a section list never read is a gap.

Where the survey publishes a response dataset, the dataset is a source of a different kind — it
supports a claim the published sections do not make, but only if the derivation is recorded.
Wave 1 admits the **published section**; a derived cut from the raw responses is an analysis, not
a retrieved finding, and does not belong in a search output.

## Unique coverage

Self-reported practice at a sample size no academic study reaches, for a population
systematically absent from consumer UX research. Nothing else in this registry reaches tens of
thousands of respondents, and nothing else reaches developers as subjects rather than as authors.

## Failure modes

- **Treating self-report as observation.** The whole corpus is self-reported, which bounds what
  any finding from it can support. The record must not read as behavioural evidence.
- **Reading one wave as a trend.** A single wave is a point in time. A trend claim needs two
  waves and the record must name both.
- **Deriving a cut and presenting it as a published result.** See above — wave 1 admits published
  sections.
- **Padding to fill the cap.** Eight is already generous for one survey against one scope. The
  proportionality condition applies with full force here: a thin honest result is the correct
  output, and there is no larger corpus behind this source to reach for.

## Fallback

`crossref`. A single annual survey is a single point of failure; the general index reaches the
academic developer-experience literature — which is small, but real — when the survey is
unavailable or its wave has not yet published.

## Cap

8, the smallest in the registry and deliberately so. The angle walks ONE published survey and its
dataset. Eight is the number of result sections a single wave carries that can map onto one
scope's task terms; anything larger would be padding from a corpus that does not exist.
