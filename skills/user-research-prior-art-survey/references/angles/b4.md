# b4 — human-AI interaction guideline retrieval

**Trigger:** conditional — `data_ml.ml_involvement != "none"`. **Anchor:**
`data_ml.ml_involvement` (required). **Cap:** 25. **Sources:** `arxiv-listing`, `crossref`.
**Fallback:** `crossref`.

## Mechanism

Retrieve published human-AI interaction research and the guideline sets derived from it, for the
scope's `task` vocabulary — how people calibrate trust in a probabilistic component, how they
recover when it is wrong, and how they read a confidence signal.

The preprint listing carries this literature first and the general index carries its
peer-reviewed subset later, so the two sources are a recency/scrutiny pair rather than
alternatives. A record from the listing is admissible; its provenance is simply what it is.

## Query strategy

Cross each `task` term with the interaction concept, not with the model technology. "Trust
calibration" plus the task retrieves interaction work; the model architecture's name retrieves
systems papers with no user in them.

The listing declares a 15-second crawl delay. Traverse the subject listing, shortlist from the
titles, and record the `selection` — including what was identified and not fetched.

## Unique coverage

The failure modes that exist only because a component is probabilistic. No conventional usability
finding covers what happens when a control is right most of the time, and a register without
this angle will describe the interface as though its outputs were deterministic.

## Failure modes

- **Retrieving systems papers.** The large majority of work under an AI subject classification
  has no human participant. A paper with no user study states no finding this survey can use,
  and its full text being open does not change that — the admission's second conjunct is a
  stated method, and a model benchmark is not one.
- **Treating a guideline set as evidence.** A published set of human-AI interaction guidelines is
  a synthesis. It is admissible where it states the studies behind it and is a secondary source
  where it does not; the record must say which.
- **Ignoring the subject classification's self-assignment.** Authors choose their own
  classification on the preprint listing, so the subject slice is noisy in both directions.
  Expect it, record it, do not compensate by widening the query.
- **Firing on a scope with no model in it.** The trigger rests on a required field; `none` means
  the angle does not run.

## Fallback

`crossref`. The listing is crawl-delayed and self-classified; the general index reaches the
peer-reviewed subset of the same literature by DOI, with the reviewing filter already applied.

## Cap

25. The subject slice is large but the 15-second delay on the listing bounds what a run can
retrieve — about six minutes of listing traversal plus the index queries.
