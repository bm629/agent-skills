# b2 — platform-regulation + compliance-delegation retrieval

**Trigger:** conditional — `business.platform.type in {marketplace, app-store, payments-network}`
**OR** `regulatory.applies`. **Anchors (required-rooted, both):** `business.platform.type`,
`regulatory.applies`. **Cap:** 10 — **ordering:** instrument bindingness, then jurisdiction reach.
**Sources:** `stripe-connect`, `apple-review`, `play-policies`. **Fallback:**
`eur-lex-dma-summary`.

## Why this predicate is not a tautology

The first leg names **three of the seven** live values, so it discriminates. The second leg is
cross-cluster and rests on `regulatory.applies`, which is required — so the predicate always
evaluates and neither leg is a restatement of the type trigger.

**Falsifying witness:** `business.platform.type = data-platform` with `regulatory.applies = false`
satisfies the type trigger and leaves this angle false.

## Mechanism

Retrieve the regulatory obligations a platform carries and, more importantly, **what it delegates
to complementors** — gatekeeper duties, payment rules, tax handling, compliance pass-through. The
delegation boundary is the finding: it tells a downstream architecture which obligations arrive
with the platform and which it must discharge itself.

## A fallback that did not resolve

This angle previously named a legislative full-text endpoint as its fallback. That endpoint returns
**HTTP 202 with a zero-byte body** to every client tried, twice, including with a cookie jar — so
it was carried as a fallback while resolving to nothing. It is replaced by the summary route. If
that also degrades, **record a zero with its cause rather than inventing a source.**
