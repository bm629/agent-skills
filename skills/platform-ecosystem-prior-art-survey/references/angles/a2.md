# a2 — marketplace program-policy + commercial-terms retrieval

**Trigger:** always applicable. **Cap:** 12 — **ordering:** bindingness (developer agreement >
program policy > guidance), then recency. **Sources:** `cws-policies`, `apple-review`,
`mozilla-policies`, `wp-guidelines`, `play-policies`, `hubspot-listing`, `shopify-revshare`.
**Fallback:** `chromium-ext-group`.

## Mechanism

Read the **binding** developer agreement and program policy for each marketplace: revenue split,
review gates, delisting powers, what the platform reserves the right to change unilaterally.

Bindingness is the ordering because it is a real property of the text, not a preference for
official sources. Normative content is prescriptive; informative content assists understanding.
The distinction is asserted by the original author in place and does not survive extraction into
someone else's prose.

## The absence of a number is a FINDING

**Record an absence as evidence, not as a gap.** "Apple's guidelines state no commission rate"
is a fact about the corpus; leaving the field empty is a hole someone will later read as an
oversight. That is what the `finding` field is for.

**Which absences are worth recording.** The test is whether a rate WOULD arise. A platform that
takes a cut and does not publish the number has made a decision, and the silence is evidence about
it. A free directory that charges nothing has no rate to state, and its silence is evidence about
nothing. Judge by the platform, not by the page: `play-policies` is a policy page carrying no
figure, and Google Play is emphatically a paid marketplace — that absence counts.

Three rows in this corpus are known to be of the first kind: `apple-review` (yours), plus
`atlassian-marketplace` and `github-marketplace`, which are a1's — named so you know the corpus
count is three, counted once, and not "three and two more". **That is not a cap.** If you retrieve
a fourth, record it; the three are what a previous pass confirmed, not a quota.

## `as_of` where the page is undated

Apple's guidelines **gained a date**: `apple-review` now carries `as_of: 2026-06-08`, and the
earlier "carries no date, self-describes as living" is false. Read the registry row, not this
sentence — it stayed wrong for eleven days, which is the argument for the row being the single
source of a source's facts.

The rule this case was written to teach still holds where a normative page genuinely carries no
date: cite the normative text for the FACT and the separate dated channel (`apple-dev-news`) for
the DATE, record both, and never default an undated policy to the fetch date.

## Unique coverage

The commercial and governance terms. No other angle reads what the platform obliges a complementor
to accept.
