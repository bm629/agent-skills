# Angle b2 — vendor and service advisory retrieval

**Precondition:** the product names third-party services, hosted platforms or cloud providers.
Where the scope names none, this angle does not run and is recorded as `not_run` with that
cause — the report then states that the vendor-advisory surface was not covered and why, never
that no vendor advisories were found.

**Mechanism.** Fetch first-party machine-readable advisories for each named service. First-party
is the point: only the vendor can say a product is *not* affected, and that statement exists
nowhere else.

**Sources:** `csaf-microsoft`, `aws-bulletins`, `google-cloud-bulletins`, `openvex-feeds`.

**Applicable group types:** `vendor-product`.

**Cap:** 30 advisories. **Ordering signal:** affected-service match, then recency.

## Query strategy

1. Take the named service set from the `vendor-product` groups.
2. For each vendor, resolve its advisory channel from the registry and fetch. Channels differ in
   kind, not just in address: some publish a standard machine-readable advisory format, some a
   syndication feed, some only human-readable bulletin pages. Record which you used.
3. Filter to advisories affecting a service the product actually names — a vendor's advisory for
   an unrelated product line is not this product's finding.
4. Capture exploitability statements where the vendor publishes them. A first-party "not
   affected" against a version the product uses is a genuine result and belongs in the output;
   it is evidence, not an empty hand.
5. Record each as a `registry` candidate under the vendor's advisory identifier, with
   `authority_band: vendor-advisory`.

## Unique coverage

First-party not-affected statements, and vendor-specific advisories that never reach a
third-party aggregator. For a product built on managed services, this is the only angle covering
the parts of its attack surface it does not operate.

## Failure modes specific to this angle

- **Reading an embargoed bulletin as an absence.** At least one major cloud provider publishes
  bulletins saying only "security update" until an embargo lifts, then amends them with the
  detail. A bulletin read during embargo is **incomplete, not absent** — that is the
  `embargoed-placeholder` cell status, and recording it as a reached zero produces a confidently
  wrong coverage claim that will not be revisited.
- **Substituting a third-party summary.** A summary is `secondary-commentary`, not
  `vendor-advisory`, and it cannot carry a not-affected statement.
- **Uneven channels read as uneven diligence.** Vendors genuinely differ in what they publish;
  record what each channel offered rather than normalising it away.

## Sanitization

Every channel here is fetched, including syndication feeds and structured advisory documents.
Pass all of it through a **content-sanitization guardrail** before acting on it, recorded per
source. A machine-readable advisory format is a machine-readable format, not a trusted one.

## Fallbacks

For each vendor, its human-readable advisory or release-notes pages when the structured channel
is unavailable — a genuine degradation, since the structured form carries exploitability
statements the prose usually omits. Record it as `partial` with that cause.
