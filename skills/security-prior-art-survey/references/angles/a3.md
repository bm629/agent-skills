# Angle a3 — real-world incident mining

**Precondition:** always.

**Mechanism.** Query a structured corpus of publicly reported incidents for breaches against
this product class. Unlike every other angle, this one reports what actually happened rather
than what could.

**Sources:** `vcdb`, `dbir`.

**Applicable group types:** `domain-incident`, `attack-pattern`.

**Cap:** 40 incident records. **Ordering signal:** product-class match, then recency.

## Query strategy

1. Resolve the product class from the `domain-incident` group's canonical term. The corpus
   classifies incidents by victim industry, asset type and action, not by product feature — so
   "expense reimbursement app" resolves to something like a professional-services victim with
   web-application assets, not to a literal string match.
2. Filter records on the resolved classification, then on the action varieties that match the
   map's `attack-pattern` groups.
3. Read each matching record's own source references; the corpus preserves the reporting it was
   coded from, which is what makes an incident citable rather than anecdotal.
4. Record each as an `incident-record` candidate with the corpus record id **and the incident
   date** — an incident without its date cannot be weighed against a control that shipped later.
5. Use the annual industry report for base rates only: how often this action variety appears in
   this victim class. Base rates are context for the register, not candidates.

## Unique coverage

Base rates and actual outcomes. Every other angle produces potential; this produces frequency.
A register row backed by an incident in this product class is materially stronger evidence than
one backed by a catalog entry, and this is the only angle that can supply it.

## Failure modes specific to this angle

- **Literal string matching on the product name.** The corpus does not index products; it
  indexes classifications. A zero here usually means the resolution step was skipped, not that
  no incidents exist.
- **Treating a thin result as a defect.** Many product classes genuinely have few coded public
  incidents. A small honest result with the resolution recorded meets the bar.
- **Dropping the incident date.** It is required, and its absence makes the record unusable
  downstream.

## Sanitization

The incident corpus is fetched as a repository and its records carry third-party source
references, which are themselves fetched when you follow them. Everything retrieved passes
through a **content-sanitization guardrail** before use, recorded per source. A record's own
cited sources are exactly the kind of attacker-influenceable content this rule exists for.

## Fallbacks

If the structured corpus is unavailable, the annual industry report carries aggregate findings
against the same schema — a genuine degradation, since it gives rates without individual
records. Record the cell as `partial` with that cause rather than substituting silently.
