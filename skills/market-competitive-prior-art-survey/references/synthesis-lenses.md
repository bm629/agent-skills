# Synthesis lenses — the five corpus cuts

Load when running Procedure 4 (synthesis). The extract set (`extract/*.md`) is the corpus; each
lens is a cut ACROSS it. A report that walks the records one at a time has concatenated the survey,
not synthesised it — and clustering products into segments is precisely the work that belongs here
rather than in any single record.

## The five lenses

1. **Segmentation.** Which products cluster, and on what — capability overlap, audience, or
   approach. The tiers are per product; the segments are only visible across them.
2. **White space.** Which of the scope's capabilities no surveyed product serves well, stated as a
   search result rather than a fact about the world. "No product in this survey covers X across the
   angles that ran" is supportable; "nobody has built X" is not.
3. **Survivorship.** What the discontinued and acquired products show. This is the cut no
   competitor-comparison source performs, and it is the highest-value input to a risk section: a
   dated failure is evidence, where a live product's marketing is a claim.
4. **Pricing shape.** How the segment charges, and where this product's plausible model sits
   against it. Carry the `as_of` dates into the report — a pricing paragraph read six months later
   without its dates is worse than none.
5. **Absence.** What the survey did NOT reach, and whether that is a finding or a limit. An angle
   that vacated for want of a durable source produced no evidence about the market; an angle that
   ran and found nothing did.

## Positioning is a claim, not a fact

Every vendor describes itself favourably. Report what a vendor claims as what it claims, attributed
to it. Restating a marketing line as a finding launders it into evidence the survey never gathered.

## Phrasing an absence claim

Name what was searched and what was not: "no direct competitor surfaced across the three angles
that ran, at their 2026-08 state" — never "there are no competitors". The first is a survey result;
the second is a claim the survey cannot support.

## Grounding, not assertion

Every report sentence that makes a claim carries the product id or source it rests on. The
reviewing twin checks this, and it is the difference between a landscape and an opinion.
