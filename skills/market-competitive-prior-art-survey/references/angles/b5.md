# Angle b5 — product-mortality mining

**Precondition:** `domain.audience != internal`. An internal tool has no external market and so
no graveyard; everything else does.

**Mechanism.** Enumerate products in this space that DIED — shut down, sunset, acquired and
discontinued, or abandoned — and record the dated evidence that they did.

**Sources:** `wikidata-sparql`, `killedbygoogle`, `closedorclosing`.

**Applicable group types:** `category`, `seed-product`.

**Cap:** 15 candidates — declared in `references/source-registry.yaml`, which is the single source. **Ordering signal:** discontinuation date, most recent first.

## Why this angle exists

This is the provable-absence rule applied positively. Most of the survey fights the problem that absence of
evidence looks identical to evidence of absence. Here the evidence is POSITIVE and dated: a
discontinuation notice is a provable fact, where "nobody has built this" is an unprovable
claim no survey can make.

For a PRD, a product that died in this exact space is the highest-value input available to the
risk section and to the MVP boundary. It answers "what did someone try that did not work", which
no competitor-comparison source carries, because comparison sites drop dead products by design.

## Query strategy

1. **Wikidata SPARQL** — the systematic leg. Query items with `P31` (instance of) = `Q7397`
   (software) carrying `P2669` (discontinued date), filtered to the category terms. This
   returns dated discontinuations rather than anecdote, which is what makes the angle
   defensible. Verified live: returns bindings with dates.
2. **killedbygoogle** — read `graveyard.json` from the public git repo. Machine-readable, no
   web dependency, and precise about dates. Google products only, so it is complete for one
   vendor and silent about everyone else — say so rather than implying wider coverage.
3. **closedorclosing** — HTML only, no API, no export. Best-effort; read what the page shows
   and record the retrieval date.
4. For each dead product found, record: the name, the discontinuation date, the evidence URL,
   and — where the source states it — the stated reason. **Never infer a reason.** A shutdown
   notice that gives no reason is recorded as giving no reason.
5. Distinguish the modes, because they mean different things to a PRD: shut down entirely;
   acquired and folded into another product; open-sourced and abandoned; still running but
   unmaintained. The last is the hardest to evidence and the easiest to assert — require a
   dated signal (last release, last commit, an end-of-support notice) or do not claim it.

## Unique coverage

Negative evidence. Every other angle in this survey describes products that exist; this is the
only one that describes the space's failures, and it is structurally invisible to a1 (which
reads directories that delist dead products), b1 (aggregators drop them), and b3 (a dead
package's downloads simply decay).

## Failure modes specific to this angle

- **Inferring a cause of death.** "Shut down after failing to find product-market fit" is a
  narrative unless the source says it. Record the notice; let synthesis reason about it.
- **Reporting killedbygoogle's coverage as the market's.** One vendor's graveyard.
- **Treating quiet as dead.** A product with no release in a year may be finished, feature-
  complete, or maintained privately. Require a dated signal.
- **Missing the acquisition case.** A product that "disappeared" because its team was acquired
  is a very different lesson from one that ran out of users. b4 resolves the corporate side;
  route the lead there rather than guessing here.
- **Losing the date.** A discontinuation with no date cannot be reasoned about — is it a
  cautionary tale from last year or from 2011? The date IS the evidence.

## Sanitization

Shutdown notices, archived pages and community-maintained lists are all untrusted input. Pass
fetched content through a content-sanitization guardrail and record the result per cell.

## Fallbacks

`killedbygoogle` — a machine-readable file in a public git repo, with no web dependency and no
rate limit. It is narrow (one vendor) but it cannot fail, so the angle degrades to a real if
partial result rather than to nothing. Record the substitution and the narrowed scope in
`retrieval_summary.degraded_sources` with `fallback_used`.
