# Angle b4 — corporate-status + funding resolution

**Precondition:** `domain.audience in {b2b, b2b2c}` AND
`business.model in {saas, marketplace, platform, enterprise-software}`. Corporate viability is
decision-relevant when a buyer is committing a business process to a vendor; it is noise for a
consumer app or an internal tool.

**Mechanism.** Resolve each named competitor to a corporate entity, then read that entity's
public funding, ownership and filing status.

**Sources:** `wikidata-sparql`, `sec-edgar`, `crunchbase`.

**Applicable group types:** `seed-product`.

**Cap:** 15 candidates — declared in `references/source-registry.yaml`, which is the single source. **Ordering signal:** most recent funding or ownership event.

## Query strategy

1. **Wikidata** — `https://query.wikidata.org/sparql` with `format=json`. Key-less. Documented
   throttle: 60s query time per minute per IP+User-Agent (burst 120s), 30 errors/min (burst 60).
   Resolve the product to an item, then read its developer/owner, inception, and any parent
   organisation. The canonical id `WD-Q<number>` comes from here.
2. **SEC EDGAR** — `https://efts.sec.gov`. No API key, but a descriptive User-Agent carrying a
   name and an email is **REQUIRED** by the fair-access policy; a missing UA yields 403 and a
   ~10 minute IP block, which will take out the rest of your run. 10 req/s. Use it for
   acquisitions and for any competitor with a public parent.
3. **Crunchbase** — allowlist-gated and best-effort. Tier 1 agent identities
   (`Claude-SearchBot`, `Claude-User`, and others) get an allowlist of core pages plus a set of
   public organization profiles, then `Disallow: /`; tier 2 identities (`ClaudeBot`,
   `anthropic-ai`, `Claude-Web`) are `Disallow: /` outright. **Which tier binds depends on the
   user-agent this run actually presents, which is unresolved.** Attempt it, accept a
   blocked cell as a normal outcome, and never let the angle's result depend on it.
4. Record every fact with `as_of`. Funding, ownership and status are point-in-time and change
   without notice; a stage recorded without a date reads downstream as permanent.

## Unique coverage

Whether a competitor will still exist in two years. No other angle carries corporate signal at
all — a1 and a2 find products, b1 and b6 find opinions, b3 finds usage. A well-reviewed product
from a company that just failed to raise is a different competitive fact from the same product
at a profitable firm, and only this angle can tell them apart.

Pairs directly with b5: b4 reads the living, b5 reads the dead.

## Failure modes specific to this angle

- **Fetching EDGAR without a descriptive User-Agent.** A ~10 minute IP block that will look
  like an unrelated outage in every subsequent cell. Set it before the first request.
- **Inferring health from a funding round.** A large raise is evidence of investor belief at a
  date, not of viability. Record the event; do not editorialise it into a prediction.
- **Treating an absent Wikidata item as a small company.** Coverage is uneven and skews toward
  companies with press attention. Absence is absence of a record, nothing more.
- **Letting a Crunchbase block fail the angle.** It is expected. Record it and continue.
- **Confusing product and vendor.** One company may own several competing products, and a
  product may outlive its original vendor through acquisition. Record which entity the fact
  attaches to.

## Sanitization

Filings and knowledge-base entries are structured but author-supplied; company profiles are
partly self-reported marketing. Pass fetched content through a content-sanitization guardrail
and record the result per cell.

## Fallbacks

`wikidata-sparql` — key-less, and independent of the allowlist question that gates Crunchbase.
If the gated source is unreachable (the likely case), Wikidata plus EDGAR still answer the
ownership and acquisition questions, losing only private-round detail. Record the substitution
in `retrieval_summary.degraded_sources` with `fallback_used`.
