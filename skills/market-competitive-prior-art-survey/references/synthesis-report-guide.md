# Synthesis report — structure guide

Load when running Procedure 4. The report (`report.md`) is the project-level human half;
`competitor-register.yaml` is its machine half and the build-handoff index. The survey ships both.

## The seven sections are FIXED

The coordinator's synthesis brief demands exactly these, in this order, so guide and brief cannot
drift.

1. **Survey scope.** What was surveyed and how: the angles that ran, the sources reached, the date.
2. **Coverage and absence** *(second on purpose — it is what a reader checks before trusting the
   rest)*. Per angle: ran, vacated, or not run, each non-`ran` outcome with its cause.
3. **The landscape.** The segmentation result (lens 1): who competes, clustered, with each product's
   tier and the capabilities it overlaps.
4. **White space.** The gaps result (lens 2), phrased as a search result and never as a claim about
   the world.
5. **What failed, and when.** The survivorship result (lens 3): discontinued and acquired products
   with their dates and evidence. The section no comparison site writes.
6. **Pricing shape.** The pricing result (lens 4), every figure carrying the date it was true.
7. **Amendments changelog** *(delta runs)*. One dated entry per delta run: what changed, what was
   added, and what a prior run claimed that this one corrects.

## What does not go in the report

No recommendation about what THIS product should build, price at, or position against. The survey
reports the landscape; choosing a position within it is the downstream document's job, working from
the register. A report that picks the positioning has quietly replaced the decision it was meant to
inform.

No feature matrix as a separate artifact either — it is derived from the register, and a derived
file diverges from its source.

## Grounding

Every claim carries the product id or the source it rests on, with the date for anything
commercial. An unattributed sentence in a landscape report is an opinion wearing a citation's
clothes.
