# Sources — `reviewing-user-guide`

This skill is the **review** half of the end-user-guide pair. Its quality bar is
**single-sourced** from the authoring sibling's shared research dossier — it does
NOT run an independent research pass, so the produce-bar and the review-bar cannot
drift. The dossier was deep-research grounded (>=2 independent sources per claim);
the citations below are carried from it for provenance.

## Single-source of the bar (primary)

- **Shared user-guide dossier**, section 4 "Usability / accuracy QUALITY BAR
  (assertable checklist)" — the eight pass/fail conditions in this skill's
  Workflow Step 2 are the same conditions the user-guide author produces to.
  (Produced by the `authoring-user-guide` sibling; the review skill asserts it.)
  - Section 2 ("Canonical section set, keyed to Diataxis") backs the four-mode
    typing check (condition 2).
  - Section 3 ("Research method — deriving the guide, not inventing it") backs the
    per-goal how-to coverage rule (condition 1), the procedure mechanics
    (condition 5), the troubleshooting-from-known-error-states rule (condition 6),
    and the no-fabrication / surface-gaps-as-assumptions discipline (conditions
    4 + 8).

## Underlying authorities (carried from the dossier)

- **Diataxis framework** (diataxis.fr — start-here, tutorials, how-to, reference,
  explanation) — the four documentation modes and the core claim that **mixing
  the modes is the most common cause of confusing documentation**; grounds the
  "present AND correctly typed" check (condition 2).
- **Google developer documentation style guide** (/procedures) and **Microsoft
  Writing Style Guide** (writing step-by-step instructions) — two independent
  authorities for procedure mechanics: numbered steps, one action per step,
  second person, present tense, imperative voice, task-based headings
  (condition 5).
- **Carroll's minimalism** (task-oriented documentation) — anchor in the user's
  real tasks, support error recognition + recovery, cut everything that does not
  serve the task; grounds the usability-by-audience check (condition 7) and the
  symptom-first troubleshooting organization (condition 6).
- **User-guide practitioner sources** (ProProfs, Docsie, document360, indoc.pro,
  thegooddocsproject) — the canonical end-user section set (getting-started,
  how-to, concepts, feature/settings reference, troubleshooting/FAQ, glossary)
  and the symptom -> cause -> fix troubleshooting shape.

## Reviewer-discipline evidence (no-false-revise)

- The dossier's quality bar is framed as an **assertable pass/fail checklist** —
  a condition is a gap only on a *named, real* deficiency. This grounds the
  no-false-revise / no-false-approve discipline and the proportionality rule
  (a thin product's guide is correctly small). The well-documented reviewer
  tendency to over-correct (especially when also asked to propose fixes) is the
  reason the skill repeatedly calibrates against manufactured nits.

## Sibling skills as structural source material (synthesize-only)

- `reviewing-design-system`, `reviewing-user-flows`, `reviewing-feature-spec`,
  `reviewing-prd` — forge-made review siblings in the same document-skill
  library. Used as **source material** for the shared review-skill shape (the
  `VERDICT: approve|revise` contract, the judge-never-author separation, the
  "judge against the handed-in upstreams / a not-produced upstream is never a
  revise trigger" rule, the proportionality calibration). Paraphrased, never
  copied; this skill's bar is the user-guide dossier, not theirs.
