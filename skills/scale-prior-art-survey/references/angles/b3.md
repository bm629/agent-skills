# `b3`

Incident and post-mortem retrieval. Walks the curated index, the first-party event summaries and the provider status histories for what actually failed under load.

## Wave-0 input

`failure-class`

Its input comes from **wave 0 and its own channel only**. An angle taking its input from a sibling
angle's OUTPUT would be reading a vocabulary the sibling minted, which L-9 forbids — and
`seed_input` is the anti-drift mechanism that makes the rule checkable.

## Applicable group types

`failure-class`

The owed grid crosses these groups with this angle's own sources INTERSECTED with the map's ACTIVE
set. All three terms: dropping any one is wrong in a measurable way.

## When it runs

`precondition`: scale.availability_target in {"99.95", "99.99", "99.999"} OR scale.concurrency in {high, extreme} OR scale.burst_traffic OR scale.stateful

**`trigger_anchor`**: `scale.availability_target`, `scale.concurrency` — each a REQUIRED capability-map leaf, so the predicate cannot fail closed on a map that omits an optional field.

**`widening_legs`**: `scale.burst_traffic`, `scale.stateful` — OPTIONAL fields only.

**`predicate_omits`**: 
- scale.real_time — a latency obligation is not by itself an availability obligation, and b1 owns it.

## Cap and ordering

**`cap: 30`.** A BUDGET, and the corpus WAS measured: at least 104 load-caused entries across two of the six listings, and two of the six are unbounded time series — so no number clears the corpus and 30 stands as a budget. Its ordering is therefore load-bearing rather than decorative.

**`ordering_signal`:** Blast radius first (entries stating a wider user or region impact outrank narrower ones), then recency. Both are readable on every one of the six.

Record `bound{cap, hit, ordering, dropped_note, ordering_deviation}`. The cap is transcribed
VERBATIM from the registry — an author does not widen their own cap. `hit: true` owes a
`dropped_note` a reader could RE-APPLY: name the ordering position reached and the first row that
fell off, never "the rest were dropped".

## The sizing record

**`sizing_class: budget-floor`.** gcp-status and azure-status-history are unbounded time series and aws-post-event has no listing at all, so the floor is a floor and not a union. 104 exceeds the cap of 30, which is why the ordering is load-bearing.

Floor **104**, counted across **2** of the angle's listings. Matcher: Entries whose stated cause names load, saturation, capacity, throttling or cascading failure — applied to post-mortems-index and cloudflare-outage-tag, the two of six that expose a walkable listing.
