# `b2`

Geo-distribution and consistency evidence retrieval. Walks the literature and the independent safety analyses for what a replication topology actually guarantees.

## Wave-0 input

`named-technology`, `system-class`

Its input comes from **wave 0 and its own channel only**. An angle taking its input from a sibling
angle's OUTPUT would be reading a vocabulary the sibling minted, which L-9 forbids — and
`seed_input` is the anti-drift mechanism that makes the rule checkable.

## Applicable group types

`named-technology`, `system-class`

The owed grid crosses these groups with this angle's own sources INTERSECTED with the map's ACTIVE
set. All three terms: dropping any one is wrong in a measurable way.

## When it runs

`precondition`: scale.geo_distribution in {multi-region, global, edge} OR regulatory.applies OR scale.consistency in {strong, linearizable}

**`trigger_anchor`**: `scale.geo_distribution`, `regulatory.applies` — each a REQUIRED capability-map leaf, so the predicate cannot fail closed on a map that omits an optional field.

**`widening_legs`**: `scale.consistency` — OPTIONAL fields only.

**`predicate_omits`**: 
- scale.data_volume — volume drives partitioning, not the replication topology this angle is about.

## Cap and ordering

**`cap: 25`.** A BUDGET. The literature is unbounded through crossref and component-docs is per-vendor.

**`ordering_signal`:** Independence of the evidence first (an adversarial third-party analysis outranks a vendor claim), then publication date descending. Both readable on all five sources.

Record `bound{cap, hit, ordering, dropped_note, ordering_deviation}`. The cap is transcribed
VERBATIM from the registry — an author does not widen their own cap. `hit: true` owes a
`dropped_note` a reader could RE-APPLY: name the ordering position reached and the first row that
fell off, never "the rest were dropped".

## Two REQUIRED sub-topics, and they are TWO facts

Two capability-map gaps fold onto this angle. `infrastructure.data_residency` is a property-less
object, so a residency obligation can gate nothing (G3); and **no jurisdiction, market or country
field exists anywhere in the schema** — `jurisdiction`, `markets`, `country`, `countries`,
`locale` and `territor*` all return zero (G5).

| sub-topic | cell field | what it records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data residency | `data_residency` | the residency obligation or posture the walk found named — or `unstated` |
| Jurisdiction | `jurisdiction` | the legal regime the walk found named — or `unstated` |

**`geo_distribution` satisfies NEITHER, and conflating them is the failure this fold exists to
prevent.** It is deployment TOPOLOGY, not a legal regime: `multi-region` says a system replicates
across regions and says nothing about which regimes its data sits under, and a single-region system
can still be subject to a foreign one. Treating it as residency evidence would manufacture a
compliance claim out of an infrastructure fact.

Source them from the request context and `regulatory.frameworks` (optional — read-if-present only),
and record `unstated` when nothing names one. Same placement rule as a3's: **at least one cell**,
because the grid is closed to (group × source) and a sub-topic cannot be its own row. This angle is
CONDITIONAL, so an `outcome: not_run` artifact carries no grid at all and the rule does not reach it.
