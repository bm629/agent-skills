# Angle a2 — curated catalogs

- **Mechanism:** harvesting human-vetted catalogs — awesome lists, quality-
  ranked best-of lists, foundation landscapes, industry radars — rather than
  querying search engines. The unit of work is a catalog to walk, not a query
  to type.
- **Applicable group types:** per the registry entries for a2; broadly
  `domain`, `capability`, `technique` (catalogs are organized by
  topic/section, not by library or product-alternative).
- **Conditional:** the angle is always-on; two of its sources are
  domain-conditional (see below).
- **Sources (registry ids, tier order):** `awesome-lists`, `best-of-lists`,
  `project-awesome`, `ecosyste-ms-awesome`, `cncf-landscape` (conditional),
  `lfai-landscape` (conditional), `apache-projects`, `tech-radars`.
- **last_verified:** 2026-07-17

## Unique coverage

The established stalwarts with human vetting attached: a repo on a maintained
awesome list has passed a curator; a landscape entry carries maturity
grading; a radar blip carries an adoption verdict from practitioners. This
angle's candidates arrive pre-endorsed — record WHERE the endorsement came
from in the relevance line.

## Method shape (differs from query angles)

For each catalog: (1) FIND the right catalog(s) for the domain using the
group's canonical + expansion terms; (2) WALK the relevant sections
completely; (3) EXTRACT every entry that matches the scope (judged against
the caller's scope authority, not the section title alone). "Queries" in
your coverage cells are the catalog lookups + sections walked (e.g.
`"awesome-quant -> section: Trading & Backtesting"`), which keeps the work
reproducible.

## Source craft

### awesome-lists (tier 1)

1. Locate the domain's lists: search GitHub for `awesome <canonical>` and
   `awesome <expansion>` per domain/capability group (e.g. `awesome-quant`,
   `awesome-algotrading`), plus the master index (`sindresorhus/awesome`)
   scanned for the domain's neighborhood.
2. Judge list health before harvesting: last commit, open PR pile, link-rot
   sampling (click 3-5 entries). A dead list still gets harvested — its
   entries just carry more fossils; note the list's staleness.
3. Walk ALL sections whose titles or contents match in-scope capabilities —
   not just the obviously-named one; curators file things oddly.
4. Extract per entry: repo URL, the list's own one-line description (this is
   curated data — capture it verbatim into `description` if the repo page
   lacks one), and the list + section as the source detail in your relevance
   line.
5. Section headings are free vocabulary — record unfamiliar recurring terms
   in `notes.vocabulary_discoveries`.

Worked example (`domain` group "trading bot"): find `awesome-quant`; walk
sections "Trading & Backtesting", "Crypto", "Algorithmic Trading"; extract
~30 entries; note discovered terms "portfolio optimization", "order book".

### best-of-lists (tier 1)

`best-of-lists/best-of-*` repos are auto-ranked weekly by a quality score
computed from GitHub + package-manager signals — a free pre-screening.

1. Find the domain's best-of (`best-of-python` niches, `best-of-ml-python`,
   domain-specific ones) via GitHub search `best-of <term>`.
2. Harvest ranked sections; capture the rank position in your relevance line
   ("best-of-quant rank 4 in backtesting section").
3. The ranking is popularity-weighted — it serves Pass-1 style ranking; your
   no-floor duty here is to harvest the LOW-ranked tail of relevant sections
   too, not just the top.

### project-awesome (tier 2)

`project-awesome.org` — a browsable mirror-index of awesome lists. Use it to
CATCH lists the GitHub search missed (different discovery surface, same
underlying lists). One lookup per domain group; harvest only lists not
already walked (record "already covered via awesome-lists" as the cell note
when so — the cell still exists).

### ecosyste-ms-awesome (tier 2)

`awesome.ecosyste.ms` indexes awesome lists programmatically (lists, their
entries, metadata). Use it as the machine-assisted sweep: look up the
domain's topic, pull the entry lists, diff against what manual walking
found. Particularly good at surfacing small, newer lists. API is open; be
polite (1 req/s).

### cncf-landscape (tier 2 — conditional: cloud-native/infra-adjacent domain)

`landscape.cncf.io` — categorized, maturity-graded (graduated/incubating/
sandbox), with stars/funding/velocity per entry.

- Walk the categories matching in-scope capabilities; capture maturity in
  the relevance line ("CNCF incubating").
- Sandbox entries are exactly the small-but-real candidates the no-floor
  rule protects — harvest them all.
- When the condition doesn't hold, the map's `sources.skipped` should carry
  it with a reason.

### lfai-landscape (tier 2 — conditional: AI/data-adjacent domain)

`landscapeapp.cncf.io/lfai` — same engine, AI & Data ecosystem. Same craft
as cncf-landscape. Both landscapes double as vocabulary sources (their
category names are the community's taxonomy — record novel ones).

### apache-projects (tier 3)

`projects.apache.org` — by-category directory of ASF projects. One category
walk per domain group. ASF projects are mature/governance-heavy; even one
hit is a strong architecture reference. Zeros are common outside
data/infra domains — record them.

### tech-radars (tier 3)

Thoughtworks Technology Radar (+ the community radar index at setchy.io) —
quadrants (Tools/Platforms/Languages & Frameworks) × rings
(Adopt/Trial/Assess/Hold).

1. Search the current + previous 2-3 radar volumes for every canonical term
   and strong expansion (the radar's own search box; volumes are also PDFs).
2. A blip maps to a candidate only when it is an OSS artifact with a repo —
   resolve the blip name to its repository (one extra lookup); commercial
   blips are recorded in notes as competitor intelligence for the caller,
   not as candidates.
3. Capture the ring verdict in the relevance line ("TW Radar vol 33:
   Trial") — an expert adoption signal downstream scoring can use.
4. `Hold` blips are still candidates — a Hold verdict is exactly the
   failure-mode evidence later waves want; say so in the relevance line.

## Coverage duties

Cells for every applicable (group × active a2 source). For catalogs, a
zero-hit cell means "no relevant catalog/section exists for this group at
this source" — record the lookup attempted (e.g. `"searched: awesome
mean-reversion — no list exists"`). That zero is common for `technique`
groups (catalogs organize by domain more than technique) and it is fine.

## Failure modes and fallbacks

- Link-rot inside catalogs: a dead entry link is skipped silently UNLESS the
  project name looks load-bearing — then one resolution attempt (search the
  name on the a1 host) and record the outcome in notes as a lead for the
  caller (do NOT run a1's mechanism beyond that single name resolution).
- A catalog platform down: registry `fallbacks` name the alternate index;
  the cell records what was attempted either way.
- Duplicate harvesting across lists: expected — the merge dedups; your
  `found_by.sources` per candidate honestly lists each catalog that carried
  it (that IS curated convergence signal).

## Candidate quality hints

Curation level belongs in the relevance line: "on 3 maintained lists +
best-of rank 7" ranks above "on one stale list, last commit 2019". Never
drop the stale-list entry — flag its staleness and let screening decide.

## Worked end-to-end example (one group, one source)

Group `kw-dom-trading-bot` × source `awesome-lists`:

1. Locate: GitHub search `awesome quant`, `awesome trading`, `awesome
   algotrading` → candidate lists `wilsonfreitas/awesome-quant` (maintained,
   last commit recent), `awesome-algotrading` (stale 2021 — still walked,
   staleness noted).
2. Health check recorded: `awesome-quant: active, ~200 entries; link-rot
   sample 0/5 dead`.
3. Walk sections: "Trading & Backtesting", "Crypto", "Data Sources" (scope
   says market-data capability exists → in scope), skip "Books/Courses"
   (not code).
4. Extract ~25 repo entries; each `description` = the list's curated
   one-liner; `found_by.sources: [awesome-lists]`; relevance lines cite list
   + section.
5. Cell recorded: queries = `"gh search: awesome quant/trading/algotrading;
   walked awesome-quant sections: Trading & Backtesting, Crypto, Data
   Sources; awesome-algotrading (stale): all sections"`, timestamp,
   result_count = entries extracted.
6. Vocabulary notes: section headings gave `portfolio optimization`,
   `order book` → notes.vocabulary_discoveries.

## Catalog-health rubric (apply before every harvest)

| Signal | Healthy | Degraded (note it) |
|---|---|---|
| last commit | < 6 months | > 18 months |
| PR/issue pile | small, triaged | dozens ignored |
| link-rot sample (5 links) | 0-1 dead | 2+ dead |
| entry descriptions | present, specific | bare URLs |

Degraded catalogs still get harvested — the health note rides in the cell so
screening can weight the endorsement accordingly.

## Radar-volume walkthrough (tech-radars)

For canonical "trading bot" and technique terms: search TW Radar vols 31-33.
Typical outcome: zero direct blips (domain too niche) — record the zero
cells. For infra-adjacent scopes the yield is real: a blip like "Backstage"
(Adopt) resolves via its site to `backstage/backstage` and the relevance
line carries "TW vol 32: Adopt". Commercial-only blips (no OSS repo) go to
notes as competitor intelligence, not candidates — one line each.

## Landscape walkthrough (conditional sources)

When `cncf-landscape` is active: open the category matching each in-scope
capability (e.g. Observability), harvest EVERY card — graduated AND sandbox
— capturing maturity + stars from the card; resolve each card to its repo
URL (the card links it). ~2 minutes per category; record categories walked
in the cell's query string. The same walkthrough applies to
`lfai-landscape` with its AI/data categories.
