# Angle a5 — competitor/alternative directories

- **Mechanism:** querying alternatives-directories and product catalogs with
  the NAMES of commercial/known competitors — "open-source alternative to
  <X>" — plus walking self-hosted/product directories by category. Finds
  whole PRODUCTS positioned against what the caller's idea competes with,
  where other angles find libraries and components.
- **Applicable group types:** per the registry; broadly `competitor` (the
  star of this angle) and `domain` (category walks).
- **Conditional:** none — always-on angle (thin `competitor` groups just
  shrink it honestly).
- **Sources (registry ids, tier order):** `alternativeto`,
  `awesome-selfhosted`, `openalternative`, `selfh-st`, `libhunt`,
  `stackshare`, `product-hunt`.
- **last_verified:** 2026-07-17

## Unique coverage

Whole-product prior art with competitive positioning attached. When the
scope says "like TradingView but self-hosted", this angle answers it
literally — every hit arrives pre-labeled as an alternative to a named
competitor, which is exactly the relevance framing downstream design wants.

## Inputs this angle leans on

The map's `competitor` groups (names verbatim from the caller's context +
expansions naming adjacent products) and `domain` groups (for category
walks). If the map has NO competitor groups, work the domain categories and
record in notes that competitor-anchored queries had no material — a map
gap worth surfacing, not improvising around.

## Source craft

### alternativeto (tier 1)

`alternativeto.net` — the largest alternatives graph.

1. Per competitor term: open the product's page →
   `alternativeto.net/software/<slug>/` → filter the alternatives list by
   License: Open Source (+ Platform: Self-Hosted when the scope wants it).
2. Worked example (`competitor` group "TradingView"):
   - `alternativeto.net/software/tradingview/?license=opensource` — harvest
     every entry: name, the site's like-count (a popularity signal — goes
     in the relevance line, not `signals.stars`), and the linked homepage.
   - Resolve each entry to its source repository (the entry page usually
     links it; else ONE name lookup on the code host — record "resolved by
     name").
3. Reverse direction: when a strong OSS candidate is already known (a seed),
   open ITS alternativeto page → "alternatives to it" surfaces siblings —
   one hop only, recorded in the cell.
4. Category browse per `domain` group when the competitor list is thin:
   `alternativeto.net/category/<relevant>/ ?license=opensource`.
5. Signals: the directory's own metadata is sparse — take repo signals from
   the resolved host page, `as_of` stamped then.

### awesome-selfhosted (tier 1)

The `awesome-selfhosted` catalog — category-organized, entries carry
license + language + a one-liner.

1. Walk the categories matching the domain/capabilities (e.g. "Money,
   Budgeting & Management" for finance scopes); harvest every FOSS entry.
2. Entry format gives you `description`, `language`, license verbatim —
   capture them; resolve the repo link directly.
3. Worked example (`domain` "trading bot"): category walk yields adjacent
   whole-products (portfolio trackers, finance dashboards) — judge each
   against the caller's scope authority; borderline entries get recorded
   with an honest relevance line ("adjacent: portfolio tracker, overlaps
   analytics capability only").
4. The list is PR-reviewed and actively maintained — a strong endorsement;
   say "on awesome-selfhosted" in the relevance line.

### openalternative (tier 2)

`openalternative.co` — curated OSS-alternatives-to-proprietary directory.

1. Per competitor term: its alternatives search; entries link repos directly
   and carry stars/license snapshots (verify on the host page; the
   directory's numbers lag — your `as_of` covers this).
2. Also walk its category page per domain group; smaller catalog than
   alternativeto but higher curation and OSS-only by construction.
3. Its "stacks" pages (tech behind each listed product) are extraction-wave
   fodder — note interesting ones in notes, don't chase.

### selfh-st (tier 2)

`selfh.st/apps` — a maintained self-hosted apps directory.

1. Category/tag walk per domain group; per-entry links resolve to repos.
2. Overlaps awesome-selfhosted heavily — expected; found_by records both,
   the convergence is signal.
3. Its "new additions" feed catches YOUNG projects the older catalogs
   haven't merged yet — exactly no-floor material; walk the recent pages.

### libhunt (tier 3)

`libhunt.com` (per-language portals, e.g. python.libhunt.com) — alternatives
+ trending, mined from mentions.

1. Per competitor OR strong OSS seed: its libhunt page lists "alternatives
   and similar projects" — harvest with the mention-count in the relevance
   line.
2. Per domain group: the topic page + trending tab (short-window trending =
   young-project discovery).
3. Data is mention-derived and noisy — treat rankings as hints; the repo
   resolution and honest signals do the real work.

### stackshare (tier 3)

`stackshare.io` — what companies say they use.

1. Per competitor term: the tool's page → "alternatives" tab → filter to
   OSS entries (resolve repos).
2. Per strong candidate: its page's "companies using this" count is an
   adoption signal for the relevance line.
3. Coverage of niche domains is thin — zeros are normal, recorded.

### product-hunt (tier 3)

`producthunt.com` — launch announcements.

1. Search per competitor term + "open source", and per domain canonical
   term; filter/scan for launches whose tagline or maker comments link a
   repository.
2. Product Hunt catches BRAND-NEW products months before catalogs do — the
   young-tail complement to a2's stalwarts.
3. Launch upvotes go in the relevance line; signals from the resolved repo
   page as always.
4. Many launches are closed-source SaaS — those are notes-material
   (competitor intelligence), not candidates.

## Coverage duties

Cells per applicable (group × active a5 source), where applicability is
exactly each source's `group_types` in the registry — check it, never
assume from this prose. Zero-hit cells record the lookup attempted
("alternativeto page for X exists but zero OSS-licensed alternatives").

## Failure modes and fallbacks

- Directory pages exist but resolution links rot: one name lookup on the
  code host per entry (recorded); unresolvable entries go to notes as named
  leads rather than candidates.
- A directory is down: registry fallbacks cover the overlap
  (alternativeto ↔ openalternative; awesome-selfhosted ↔ selfh-st); the
  cell records the outage either way.
- Competitor name ambiguity (generic words): use the directory's
  autocomplete/slug to confirm the right product before harvesting; wrong
  product = wasted harvest and polluted output.
- SaaS-only results for a competitor: record the zero for OSS alternatives
  — that itself is market intelligence (note it: "no OSS alternative to X
  exists" is a finding the caller's gaps analysis wants).

## Candidate quality hints

This angle's relevance lines should name the competitor relationship
explicitly ("positioned as OSS alternative to TradingView on 2
directories") — that framing is unique to a5 and exactly what downstream
borrow-vs-build reasoning consumes. Directory popularity numbers (likes,
upvotes, mentions) are context for the line, never `signals` fields.

## Worked end-to-end example (one competitor group, full source sweep)

Group `kw-comp-tradingview` (canonical "TradingView"; expansions
"MetaTrader", "QuantConnect", "TradeStation"):

1. alternativeto: `software/tradingview` → License: Open Source → 9 entries;
   each resolved to a repo (7 direct links, 2 by-name lookups noted);
   like-counts into relevance lines. Repeat for each expansion name —
   MetaTrader's page yields the algo-trading-adjacent alternatives.
2. openalternative: search "tradingview" → 3 entries (2 overlap
   alternativeto — found_by records both; 1 new).
3. libhunt: tradingview page → "alternatives and similar" → mostly chart
   libraries — judged against scope: charting is in-scope capability →
   harvested with capability-scoped relevance lines.
4. stackshare: tradingview alternatives tab → OSS subset thin (1 hit) +
   zero for TradeStation — cells record both.
5. product-hunt: search `"open source" trading` + `tradingview alternative`
   → 2 launches with linked repos (young projects, low signals, honest
   `as_of`); 5 SaaS launches → notes as competitor intel.
6. Net: ~14 unique whole-product candidates, every one carrying an
   "alternative to <named competitor>" relevance line.

## Category-walk worked example (domain group)

Group `kw-dom-trading-bot` × awesome-selfhosted + selfh-st:

- awesome-selfhosted: category "Money, Budgeting & Management" (12 entries
  walked; 3 in-scope, 4 adjacent-recorded, 5 out-of-scope skipped —
  skips counted in the cell's query note, not silently);
- selfh-st: tag walk `finance` + new-additions feed (2 young projects the
  catalogs lack).

## Scope-judgment rubric for adjacent products

Directories return whole products; many only OVERLAP the scope. Record an
adjacent product when it implements ≥1 in-scope capability; its relevance
line names exactly which ("overlaps: portfolio analytics only"). Skip (and
count) products with zero capability overlap. The caller's scope authority (a
capability map or equivalent scope context) judges — when unsure, record
with an honest "borderline" marker rather than dropping; screening owns
the cut.
